## 2. Likely Viva Examination Questions & Precise Technical Answers

### Q1: Is this cluster running on physical servers or virtualized?
> **Answer:** "It is a logical three-node cluster running inside Docker containers on a single physical host machine using Windows 11 and WSL2. However, it is not a mock: each node runs genuine Apache Hadoop and Apache Spark daemons (`NameNode`, `DataNode`, `ResourceManager`, `NodeManager`, `SparkWorker`) communicating over container network boundaries via real RPC ports with HDFS block replication ($R=2$) and YARN memory quotas."

### Q2: How did you prevent data leakage in your real-data machine learning pipeline?
> **Answer:** "We implemented five strict anti-leakage protocols:
> 1. Strictly chronological temporal splitting: Train (2015–2021), Validation (2022–2023), Holdout Test (2024). Never random k-fold shuffling.
> 2. Feature scaler isolation: `StandardScaler` was fitted strictly on the Training partition; Validation and Test were transformed using those fixed training parameters.
> 3. Panel boundary purging: We purged boundary observations on 2021-12-31 and 2023-12-31 so that next-day targets did not peer into subsequent partitions.
> 4. Decoupling: Targets were computed via strict lead shifts (`shift(-1)`) and immediately dropped from the feature matrix.
> 5. Scale invariance: Nominal prices (`close`, `high`, `low`) were omitted; all 14 features are stationary ratios, percentages, or rolling statistics."

### Q3: Why is your real-data directional accuracy around 50.7% to 51.1% rather than 80% or 90%?
> **Answer:** "The tested models show limited out-of-sample predictive performance on the selected historical NIFTY 50 universe. These findings do not establish an exploitable trading edge and neither prove nor disprove the Efficient Market Hypothesis. While execution frictions such as transaction taxes (STT), brokerage fees, and execution slippage were not empirically modeled, their presence in live trading environments typically erodes marginal statistical edges of this magnitude. Reported accuracies exceeding 65% on daily liquid large-cap equities almost certainly suffer from data leakage (such as random train/test split or look-ahead bias). Our 50.74% accuracy on unseen 2024 data represents a genuine statistical tendency over the majority baseline (50.64%)."

### Q4: Why can't we randomly split stock data using `train_test_split(shuffle=True)`?
> **Answer:** "Randomly splitting stock data violates temporal ordering and creates severe data leakage. Financial time series exhibit serial autocorrelation. If day $t$ is in the test set while days $t-1$ and $t+1$ are in the training set, the model interpolates between known past and future points rather than truly forecasting unseen future events."

### Q5: Why do we predict percentage return instead of absolute stock price?
> **Answer:** "First, stationarity: raw stock price levels exhibit non-stationary random walks, whereas percentage returns are roughly stationary. Second, cross-stock scale comparability: a ₹50 move on a ₹100 stock is a massive 50% shift, whereas on a ₹7,000 stock it is under 1%. Third, models predicting raw prices often learn the trivial identity $\hat{P}_{t+1} \approx P_t$, producing deceptive 99% $R^2$ scores that have zero forecasting value."

### Q6: Why did you standardize features before K-Means clustering?
> **Answer:** "K-Means uses Euclidean distance. In raw market data, trading volume is on the scale of millions, while returns and volatilities are small decimals. Without standardization via `StandardScaler`, distance calculations would be completely dominated by volume, making price return and volatility features effectively invisible."

### Q7: How did you reconcile the 8 missing benchmark dates in Step 8.1.1?
> **Answer:** "We examined official NSE holiday circulars for each date. For example, 2019-10-27 and 2020-11-14 were special 1-hour Diwali Muhurat trading sessions where individual equities traded but Yahoo Finance omitted benchmark index aggregates. Other dates had genuine holiday circulars or source availability gaps. We preserved analytical consistency by utilizing a strict inner join on the 2,459 mutually verified trading dates."

### Q8: What is the difference between peak-to-recovery and trough-to-recovery drawdown duration?
> **Answer:** "Peak-to-recovery measures the total calendar days from the prior high-water mark peak until the asset price re-attains that level. Trough-to-recovery measures only the recovery leg, from the lowest point of the cycle (the trough) until the previous peak is regained. For example, in Tata Motors (`TATAMOTORS`), the peak-to-recovery duration was 3,082 calendar days (from Feb 2015 to July 2023), whereas the trough-to-recovery duration from the COVID trough in March 2020 was 1,189 calendar days."

### Q9: Does news sentiment have a causal impact on equity returns in your findings?
> **Answer:** "No. In our empirical testing over 67 verified corporate announcements matched with active trading days in CY 2024 (out of 79 total acquired records), the Pearson correlation with same-day returns was $+0.0888$ ($p = 0.4751$) and with next-day returns was $-0.1471$ ($p = 0.2348$). Because both $p$-values significantly exceed $\alpha = 0.05$, there is no statistically significant linear association. We formally document that news disclosures cannot be interpreted as causal drivers of price changes in this sample."

### Q10: Why use HDFS Parquet format instead of CSV for distributed storage?
> **Answer:** "Parquet is a columnar, binary storage format that implements dictionary encoding, run-length encoding, and snappy compression. In Spark queries that filter by specific columns (like `symbol` or `date`), Parquet enables columnar projection pruning and predicate pushdown, reading only the necessary byte offsets from disk rather than scanning full CSV lines, resulting in up to 10× faster query execution and 75% lower disk usage."
